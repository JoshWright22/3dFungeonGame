1

The pitch

The stairs end in a room the map does not have. Someone's torch is already down to a stub. Behind you, at the edge of hearing, your cleric is explaining to nobody in particular that he rolled a seven for Strength and cannot open the door.

You have eleven minutes before the way out closes.

Party Wipe is a four-player cooperative dungeon crawl that takes the shape of a tabletop delve — rolled stats, a dungeon master's grid, loot you have to physically carry out — and runs it at the pressure and tempo of a shift-based horror co-op. The comedy and the horror come from the same source: you can hear each other, positionally, all the time, and nobody is good at their job.

What it is

  • Short runs — one dungeon, one extraction window, roughly twenty minutes.
  • Character sheets rolled fresh each run. No builds, no loadouts, no meta.
  • Physical inventory: two hands, and what's in them is what you have.
  • Voice as a mechanic. Distance, walls and torchlight all change what you can hear.

What it isn't

  • Not a combat game first. Fights are a failure state you sometimes survive.
  • Not a progression treadmill. Meta-unlocks widen the content pool, never the power curve.
  • Not a tactics sim. There is no turn order and no grid the player can see.
2

Design pillars

Four rules. When a feature can't be justified against one of them, it doesn't ship.

I. The dice are public

Every roll that matters is announced in the world — a die clatter cue at the character's position, audible to anyone in earshot. Failure is never private, which means failure is always social. This is the pillar the whole D&D framing exists to serve.

II. Voice is the interface

There is no ping system, no map marker, no shared waypoint. Coordination happens by shouting down a corridor and hoping. Every system that could be solved with UI should be solved with the fact that people can talk to each other and don't.

III. Competence is unevenly distributed

3d6 in order, no rerolls, no point buy. Someone in the party is going to be genuinely bad, and the run has to route around them. That person is different every time.

IV. The dungeon does not scale to you

One seed builds one layout with one distribution of threats, before it knows who is coming. It doesn't rubber-band, it doesn't tune to party strength, and it will happily generate a run the party cannot win.

3

The expedition loop

One run, start to finish. Phases are a real sequence — each hands specific state to the next — and the durations are the tuning targets, not measurements.

  1. Muster 60–90 s · lobby

    Steam lobby fills to four. The host's seed is fixed and broadcast. Every player's sheet is rolled server-side and shown to the whole party at once — you see how bad everyone else got it before you see the dungeon.

  2. Descent 30 s · fixed

    The one scripted beat in the run. Party rides down together, torches lit, no threats. Exists purely so everyone is in voice range once before they scatter.

  3. Delve 8–12 min · open

    Explore, loot, avoid. The party splits almost immediately because two hands isn't enough carry capacity, and splitting is what makes proximity voice bite.

  4. The turn triggered

    A threshold event — quota met, a specific room opened, a timer — flips the dungeon's behaviour. Doors that were open close. Something starts moving toward the loudest player. This is the moment the run stops being a looting game.

  5. Extraction 2–4 min · timed

    Back to the entry stair with whatever is in your hands. Carry weight fights you. Dropping loot to run is always the correct call and nobody ever makes it.

  6. Tally 45 s · lobby

    Value extracted against quota. Survivors keep nothing but the story. Meta currency unlocks new dungeon furniture and item templates for the pool — never stats.

What abilities actually do

Not yet designed, and this is the most important open decision in the document. The scores exist and replicate correctly; nothing consumes them. The proposal:

  • STR — carry capacity before movement penalty; forcing stuck doors and lids.
  • DEX — movement speed ceiling, fall damage threshold, and whether a trap gets you.
  • INT — how much an item's description tells you. A low-INT delver reads "a dagger" where a high-INT one reads "a dagger, still warm".

INT-as-information is the pick worth taking the risk on: it makes a bad stat funny and useful to the party rather than just a debuff, and it feeds directly into the voice pillar — the party's smartest member has to describe things out loud.

5

Hands & items

There is no inventory screen. You have a left hand and a right hand. An item is one-handed or two-handed, and a two-handed item takes both.

Items are ObjectModelSO scriptable objects carrying a display name, a description string, a prefab, a size, and — the interesting field — an animation class. The animation class is the closest thing the game has to a character class, and it is decided by what you picked up:

Animation classReads asHands
CarryNot a fighter. Hauling loot.1 or 2
SwordAndShieldThe one who goes first2 × 1H
TwoHandWeaponCommitted. Cannot carry loot.2H
BowRanged, needs the corridor clear2H
WizardWhatever the staff does1H
RogueFast hands, no reach1H

The tension this creates is the core inventory decision of the run: every hand holding a weapon is a hand not carrying loot. A party that arms itself properly extracts nothing. A party that fills its hands with treasure has no answer when the turn comes.

Item descriptions

Descriptions are flavour text with mechanical consequences via INT (§4). They should be written to be read aloud badly, over voice chat, by someone who is running.

6

The dungeon

Generator3D builds a level on a Grid3D<CellType> in four stages. One grid cell is 5 world units; cells are typed None, Room, BottomFloorRoom, Hallway or Stairs.

  1. Place rooms rejection sampling

    Random BoundsInt volumes up to roomMaxSize, discarded on intersection, until roomCount is satisfied.

  2. Triangulate Delaunay3D

    Tetrahedralisation over the room centres, producing every plausible connection between neighbouring rooms without any long-distance nonsense.

  3. Select edges Prim MST + loops

    Minimum spanning tree guarantees the dungeon is fully connected, then a fraction of the discarded edges are added back. Those extra edges are what make the dungeon a place you can get lost in rather than a tree you can back out of.

  4. Carve hallways DungeonPathfinder3D · A*

    A* with a cost function that prices stairs high and already-carved hallway low, so corridors converge and share instead of running in parallel. Vertical movement is expensive by design — the dungeon wants to sprawl before it wants to descend.

Seeding and determinism

Generation is fully deterministic from a single integer. Generate(int seed) is the entry point; the component's Seeding fields decide what Start() passes it. This matters more than it sounds: the network plan replicates one integer instead of several thousand tile spawns.

The handshake, once built: host picks a seed → seed goes in the lobby data and a NetworkVariable → every client calls Generate(seed) locally → clients report generation complete → host releases spawn. Nothing about the geometry itself is ever sent.

The authority position

Movement and animation are client-authoritative. That is the correct trade for a four-player friends-only game over Steam P2P — it makes movement feel right and costs nothing that matters, because a cheater in a private lobby is only ruining their own evening. It is not a position that survives public matchmaking, and the decision should be revisited the moment strangers can join a lobby.

Things that must stay server-authoritative regardless: ability score rolls, the dungeon seed, loot spawn tables, quota accounting, and the extraction timer.

Known gap: item pickup is local-only

Picking an item up currently instantiates a copy in the local player's hand and destroys the world object on that client only. Every other player still sees the item on the floor and can take it themselves. This needs a server-authoritative pickup RPC before any multiplayer playtest is meaningful — it is the top of the roadmap for that reason.

8

Proximity voice

Not built yet. It is also the single feature the design leans on hardest, so the specification comes before the implementation choice.

Three voice modes

Push-to-talk selects a mode; the mode sets the falloff curve and how much attention it costs you. Shouting reaches the whole wing and tells the dungeon exactly where you are.

Occlusion and the dungeon

Straight distance is not enough in a dungeon made of walls. Voice should be filtered, not just attenuated, when the path is blocked: a low-pass through stone at roughly a third volume, full clarity through an open doorway. Because hallways are carved as grid cells, the generator already knows the topology — voice occlusion can query the same grid rather than raycasting per frame.

Implementation options

OptionCostFitRead
Steam Voice via Facepunch free Already in the stack Start here. SteamUser.VoiceRecord plus a Netcode RPC into a positioned AudioSource. No new dependency, no accounts, no per-seat cost. Manual work: opus framing, jitter buffering, occlusion filtering.
Dissonance ~$70 NGO integration ships Solves positional audio, jitter and codecs out of the box. The pragmatic buy if hand-rolled Steam voice stalls for more than a week.
Vivox metered Unity first-party Server-brokered and reliable, but adds a Unity Gaming Services dependency and a per-user bill to a game whose entire networking model is free P2P. Wrong shape.
9

Light & fear

The dungeon is unlit. Every light source in a run is carried by a player, which makes light a hand-slot cost like everything else (§5) and ties the horror directly to the inventory pressure.

  • Torches burn down. A run is longer than a torch. Somebody will be in the dark before extraction, and it will not be the person who planned for it.
  • Light is a beacon. Whatever the turn (§3) releases navigates toward light and sound. Being visible and being findable are the same property.
  • Dropped torches keep burning. A torch on the floor is a breadcrumb, a decoy, and a way to give up a hand slot without giving up the light.

URP forward+ with real-time shadows on carried lights only; everything else baked to nothing. The performance ceiling is four dynamic shadow-casting point lights, which is exactly the party size — a constraint worth designing into rather than around.

Fix log

Bugs found and fixed while writing this document. All are in the current commit.

Ability scores were never rolled — every stat was 0 PlayerStats.cs

PlayerStats was a plain MonoBehaviour with a method named OnNetworkSpawn(). Unity never calls that on a MonoBehaviour and there was no override keyword to catch it, so the roll silently never happened — every character in every run had STR 0, DEX 0, INT 0, HP 0.

Now a NetworkBehaviour that rolls on the server in a real OnNetworkSpawn override and replicates through NetworkVariables, so all four clients agree on everyone's sheet.

Every die rolled one short PlayerStats.cs

Random.Range(1, 6) — the integer overload is max-exclusive, so that is a d5, not a d6. Ability scores could never exceed 15, and Range(1, 4) made the HP die a d3.

Replaced with a Roll(count, sides) helper using Range(1, sides + 1). 3d6 now actually spans 3–18.

Every dungeon was the same dungeon Dungeon Generator/Generator3D.cs

random = new Random(0) — hard-coded seed. Every session generated a byte-identical layout.

Added a serialized seed, an optional randomise-on-start toggle, and a public Generate(int seed) entry point so the host can drive generation from a broadcast seed later. placedHallways is now cleared per generation, so regenerating in one session doesn't inherit the previous layout's corridors.

Joining a friend over Steam could never work Network/SteamClientConnector.cs

The join button read SteamClient.SteamId — the local player's own ID — so every client tried to connect to itself, and the SteamID input field was never read at all.

Now parses the field as a SteamID64, reports a clear error on bad input, and only tears down the menu camera once the input validates.

Accepting a Steam invite connected you twice Network/GameNetworkManager.cs

OnGameLobbyJoinRequested called StartClient, then entering the lobby fired OnLobbyEntered, which called StartClient again. Both paths also re-subscribed the connection callbacks without ever unsubscribing.

The invite handler now only joins the Steam lobby and lets OnLobbyEntered own the Netcode connection. StartClient bails out if already listening and de-duplicates its callback subscriptions.

Every client ran every other player's pickup raycast PickUpItems/PlayerPickupDetector.cs

Another dead OnNetworkSpawn() on a MonoBehaviour, so the intended owner gate never ran. It also polled Input.GetKey inside FixedUpdate, which both drops presses and repeats them, and raycast against every layer before filtering by hand.

Now resolves ownership through the parent NetworkObject at runtime, reads GetKeyDown in Update, and raycasts with a Fantasy Object layer mask. Still local-only on the item side — see §7.

.gitignore was UTF-16 and git could not read a line of it .gitignore

Git parses ignore files as bytes and does not understand UTF-16. Every rule in the file was inert, so Library/ and Temp/ — hundreds of megabytes of regenerable cache — were never actually being ignored.

Rewritten as UTF-8 with the standard Unity rule set, plus a .gitattributes wiring Unity's YAML merge tool for scenes and prefabs.

Only the scripts folder was under version control repository layout

The git repo lived at Assets/Created/Scripts/. Scenes, prefabs, ProjectSettings and the package manifest existed on one disk and nowhere else.

Promoted to the project root with history intact. Asset Store content (~1.3 GB) is gitignored with a re-import list in docs/; KinematicCharacterController stays tracked because it carries local netcode edits.

Project would not open on 6.3 — unresolvable package Packages/manifest.json

com.unity.rendering.light-transport@1.0.1 no longer ships as a built-in package in Unity 6.3; it was folded into render-pipelines.core. The explicit pin made the whole dependency graph unresolvable.

Pin removed, URP core and universal bumped 17.0.3 → 17.3.0 to match what 6000.3.23f1 actually ships, and the stale lock file cleared so everything re-resolves.

Roadmap

Ordered by what unblocks the most. Each milestone ends in something playable with three friends, because a co-op game that can't be played with friends can't be designed.

MilestoneShipsPlaytest question
M1 · Shared world Seed handshake, server-authoritative pickup RPC, networked item spawns Do four people see the same dungeon and the same loot?
M2 · Voice Steam voice over NGO into positional AudioSources; three modes; grid-based occlusion Is splitting up scary and funny, or just annoying?
M3 · The loop Quota, extraction timer, the turn, tally screen, torches that burn down Does anyone ever drop the loot to survive?
M4 · Something in the dark First enemy — navigates to light and sound, not to players Does the party go quiet on their own, without being told to?

Ability score consumers (§4) slot into M3 alongside the loop, once there is enough game for a bad Strength roll to actually cost something.

13

Open questions

Decisions that need making before the systems above harden around a guess.

  • What do ability scores buy?§4 proposes carry / speed / description detail. It is a proposal, not a decision, and everything in M3 depends on it.
  • First or third person?The item data carries both first-person and third-person hand transforms, and the player prefab has an Arms rig and a third-person camera. Right now the game is quietly trying to be both. Proximity voice and torch-carrying both read better in first person.
  • Does the party share one quota or four purses?Shared quota makes the loop cooperative. Individual purses make it a comedy. They are different games.
  • Death: spectate, or stay in voice as something else?Lethal Company's answer — dead players keep talking, and that's most of the entertainment — fits pillar II exactly. Worth stealing deliberately rather than by default.
  • Public lobbies, ever?Currently lobby.SetPublic() with client-authoritative movement. If public lobbies are the plan, §7's authority model has to change before anyone finds it.
